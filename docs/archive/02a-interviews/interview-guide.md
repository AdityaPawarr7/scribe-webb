# Interview Guide — Notes & Meetings (Step 2a)

**Goal:** Learn how people capture, review, and act on what's said in meetings and lectures. Check whether "I lose track of decisions and action items while I'm busy participating" is a real problem, and what it looks like for other people.

**Format:** 20–30 min, 1:1, in person or video. Record only with permission. Transcribe locally (Whisper), then anonymize as P1 / P2.

**Who to talk to (pick 2 who differ):**
- P1: a student who sits in lectures, study groups, or club meetings
- P2: someone with regular work meetings (internship, job, TA, research lab)

## Ground rules (from the assignment)
1. Talk about **them**, not Scribe. Don't mention the app until the very end, if at all.
2. Ask about **past experiences**, not hypotheticals ("Tell me about the last time…", not "Would you use…").
3. **Listen more than you talk.** Aim for them speaking ~80% of the time. Silence is fine; count to 5 before filling it.

---

## 0. Opening and consent (~2 min)
> "Thanks for doing this. I'm working on a class project about how people handle meetings and lectures, and I'd like to hear about your experiences. There are no right answers. I'm not selling anything. Is it okay if I record audio so I can transcribe it? I'll delete the recording after transcribing, and nothing I write up will identify you. You can skip any question or stop at any time."

- [ ] Consent to record obtained (verbal is fine; note the time)

## 1. Warm-up: their context (~3 min)
1. What does a typical week look like for you in terms of meetings, classes, or calls?
2. Which of those matter most to you? Why those?

## 2. The last important meeting (core, ~8 min)
3. Think about the **most recent** meeting or lecture where something important was said. Walk me through it, from before it started to after it ended.
4. Did you write anything down during it? How: laptop, phone, paper, nothing?
5. **Can you show me** what you wrote? *(If they're willing, an actual artifact is worth more than any description of it. Don't record or copy its contents.)*
6. While you were writing, what was going on in the conversation? Was there a moment you missed something?
7. What happened with those notes **after** the meeting?

## 3. Action items and follow-through (~5 min)
8. Tell me about the last time you were given a task or deadline in a meeting. How did you keep track of it?
9. Tell me about a time something from a meeting fell through the cracks, for you or someone else. What happened?

## 4. Going back to old notes (~4 min)
10. When did you last go back and look at notes from an older meeting or class? What were you looking for?
11. How did you find it? How long did it take?
12. Have you ever edited, cleaned up, or deleted old notes? Tell me about that.

## 5. Tools they've tried (~4 min)
13. What apps or methods have you used for notes over the past year or two? Which ones did you stop using, and why?
14. Have you used, or been in a meeting with, an AI note-taker or transcription tool (Otter, Zoom AI, Granola, Fireflies, etc.)? Tell me about that experience.
    - *Probe:* Was anything in the summary wrong or missing? How did you notice?
15. Have you paid for any note-taking or meeting tool? What made it worth it, or not?

## 6. Recording and privacy (~3 min)
16. Think of the last time a meeting you were in was recorded. How did you find out? How did you feel about it?
17. Is there a kind of meeting where you'd never want a record kept? What makes those different?

## 7. Close (~2 min)
18. Is there anything about how you handle meetings or notes that I should have asked about but didn't?
19. Is there someone else you think I should talk to?

> "Thank you, this was really helpful."

---

## Probes to use anywhere
- "Tell me more about that."
- "Why do you think that was?"
- "What did you do next?"
- "Can you give me a specific example?"
- "How did that make you feel?" *(use sparingly)*

## Things NOT to say
- ❌ "Would you use an app that…?" (a hypothetical, plus it pitches the idea)
- ❌ "Don't you find it annoying when…?" (a leading question)
- ❌ "I'm building an open-source Granola…" (talking about your idea)
- ❌ Asking two questions in one breath

## Interviewer checklist
- [ ] Recording app tested; phone on Do Not Disturb
- [ ] Note any non-verbal moments (hesitations, laughter, "ugh")
- [ ] Right after: write 3 bullets of first impressions before transcribing
- [ ] Transcribe → anonymize → **delete the raw audio**

## Tailored follow-ups per participant
Use these only as probes after the core question, and only if the topic comes up naturally.

### P1 — studio-based coursework
- (Q3) "Walk me through your last studio critique or desk crit. What happened to the feedback you got?"
- (Q4) "Do your notes ever include sketches, photos, or marked-up images? Show me one if you're comfortable."
- (Q8) "Last time a professor asked for revisions on a project, how did you keep track of what to change?"
- (Q10) "Have you ever needed feedback from weeks earlier when working on a later iteration? How did you find it?"
- (Q16) "Have you ever recorded a crit or review? What happened?"

### P2 — lecture and lab-based coursework
- (Q3) "Walk me through your last lecture with code or derivations on the board or slides. What did you write down?"
- (Q4) "When code is on screen, do you copy it, screenshot it, or rely on the posted slides?"
- (Q8) "Last time you had a group project meeting, how were tasks split up and tracked?"
- (Q10) "Think of the last exam you studied for. Which of your notes did you actually use?"
- (Q14) "Have you used ChatGPT or another AI tool on lecture notes or recordings? Tell me about that."
